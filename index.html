02 — How we work

A clear process, start to finish

Every project moves through the same four stages — so you always know what's happening and what's next.

  1. 01

    Discovery

    We map the problem, users, and constraints, then agree on scope and success criteria before anything is built.

    ~3–5 days
  2. 02

    Design

    Wireframes and high-fidelity UI, reviewed and iterated with you before a single line of production code is written.

    ~1–2 weeks
  3. 03

    Build

    Full-stack development in focused sprints, with regular check-ins so you're seeing working software, not just status updates.

    ~2–6 weeks, depending on scope
  4. 04

    Deploy & Support

    Production deployment, monitoring, and a stability window after launch — plus ongoing support if you need it.

    ~1 week to launch, ongoing after

05 — FAQ

Before you reach out

Questions a cautious buyer usually has before hiring a small studio.

How long does a typical project take?

It depends on scope, but most custom web apps take roughly 4–8 weeks from kickoff to launch, following the Discovery → Design → Build → Deploy process above. We agree on a realistic timeline before work starts.

How does communication work during a project?

You work directly with the person building your product — no account manager or middle layer. Expect regular check-ins and updates as each milestone is hit, not just a status report at the end.

What happens after launch?

We don't disappear at go-live. There's a stability window right after launch, and ongoing support and maintenance — security updates, bug fixes, and new features — are available as a separate, scoped arrangement.

Do we have to use your exact tech stack?

React and Spring Boot are our default because they're reliable and well-supported, but we scope each project around what makes sense for you — including working with or alongside an existing stack.

How does pricing and scoping work?

Every project starts with a scoping conversation and a written brief before any commitment. Pricing is based on the actual scope of work, not a generic fixed package.
